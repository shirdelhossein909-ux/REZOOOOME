The biggest challenge: a backtest that fooled us for three months

Our old backtest showed 825% profit over 6 years, yet the live account was losing. A trade-by-trade comparison showed that the robot's logic was right and the flaw was in the backtester: when an order filled inside a 4-hour candle, it assumed the candle's high and low came after the entry. The backtest could see the future.

+77.6% → −51.2%two-year backtest, before and after fixing that one assumption
174 / 186syncs where the orders on the account matched the backtest exactly
26 → 31 / 33shared trades predicted correctly, before and after the fix
0.6%chance of the live result if the old backtest had been right

What we did: rebuilt the backtester on 1-minute data, and the robot and the backtester now share one decision engine. Today we are rebuilding the robot and re-running the backtests with this new engine.

When Iran's internet went national, the robot's server suddenly went out of reach. So I built an engine that sends every order, every cancellation with its exact reason, and the watchdog's messages, complete and readable, to the domestic messenger Bale.

Project 2: Mahak, a test bench for Persian support chatbots

first version builtnow testing on real chatbots

Mahak talks to a support chatbot the way a customer would, tests it on five levels and hands back a report card:

  1. Basics
  2. Persian as typed
  3. Multi-turn
  4. Honesty
  5. Attempts to trick it
  • Writes its questions from the company's own documents, and asks each one again with typos, colloquially and in Finglish.
  • A critical failure, such as leaking a password or a customer's details, zeroes that test; every check is compared with the previous one.
  • Tested on a real chatbot too: DeepSeek behind a one-line prompt scored 75 yet stayed below level 1, because it makes answers up; behind a careful prompt, 97. Its model judge agreed with the rule judge 98% of the time.

How I work

The scarce skill today is knowing what to build, how to prove it works, and when to doubt a result. Both projects were built with the same loop:

  1. Specifyevery rule and edge case in plain, unambiguous language
  2. Build with AIthe model writes the code; I direct the structure and decisions
  3. Test against known answerssynthetic data, a fake MetaTrader, sample chatbots
  4. Compare with realitybacktest versus live account; Mahak's judge versus people
  5. Fix and lock inevery fix ships with a test that keeps the mistake from returning
